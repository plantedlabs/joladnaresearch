## 1. Result

**The available records do not identify what happened to the 1811 Woodley, and they document no Louisiana
connection.**
* He is recorded once, in March 1811, allotted to Adin Linch.
* Twenty-nine years later, the complete sale of the enslaved people Adin Lynch held at his death (February
  1840) accounts for every other member of his 1811 lot, and does not include him.
* Neither case file explains the gap.
* Per the stop condition, this pass ends here, with one archival action recommended (§6).

## 3. Answers to the research tasks

**(1) All pages of 1811-003 examined.** All 18 pages: cover index (0001), bill (0002–0003), answer (0003),
docket (0004), decree (0005–0006), report (0007–0008), plat (0009p–0010), Clary will (0011–0012), Lots 1–2
(0013–0016) and administration account (0017–0018). Woodley appears **only** on the cover index (a 20th-century
archivist's list) and on Lot No. 2 (0013).

**(2) Who the parties were and the legal basis of Lot No. 2.**
* **Judith Jones** died intestate in 1809 in Brunswick County.
* **Adin (Aden) Linch/Lynch** was her son-in-law, husband of Salley Jones, and her administrator.
* Lot No. 2 was **half of the enslaved people of Judith Jones's intestate estate**. It was assigned to Adin
  Linch in a consent division between the two heirs: Salley Linch and the infant Patsey J. Brock.

**(3) What the allotment conveyed.**
* *Confirmed (text):* "Lott No. 2 drawn by Adin Linch **as his share** of the Negroes belonging to the Est. of
  Judith Jones" (img 0014).
* *Plausible interpretation (law, not stated in the file):* this was a distributive share of personal property
  from an intestate estate. Under Virginia law of the time, enslaved people were distributed as personal
  property, and a married woman's share of personal property vested in her husband. So this most likely
  conveyed **full ownership to Adin Linch**, not a life interest.
* The land was different. Under Clary's will Judith had only a life estate, and Adin later held his wife's land
  "by Curtesy" (1850-003, img 0003).

**(4) What happened to the 1811 people (reconstructed from 1850-003):**

| 1811 (Lot No. 2) | Feb 1840 (Adin Lynch's estate) |
|---|---|
| Butcher (£110) | sold to Grief H. Lynch, $215 |
| Solomon (£90) | sold to John Owen, $661 |
| Joe (£70) | sold for cash to Clement Hall, $599 |
| Violet (£55) | sold to Sterling C. Pearson, $27 (the low price suggests she was elderly; inference only) |
| **Woodley (£15)** | **not present** |
| Lucey (£95) | "Lucy", sold with Nelly to Sterling C. Pearson. *Possibly* a different, younger Lucy, since she is listed as Nelly's child; unresolved |
| German (£18) | sold for cash to Isaac F. House, $640 |
| Nelley (£50) | "Woman Nelly & two children Lucy & Jane", to Sterling C. Pearson, $515 |

New people in 1840 (Peggy, Susan, Tillman, Allan, Jane) were probably born after 1811 or acquired later. That
is an inference.

**(5) Adin Lynch's will, inventory, accounts and transfers, c. 1830–1845.**
* The will exists: it was "duly admitted to record in this Court" before January 1840. The copy marked "A" is
  **not** among the 22 scanned pages.
* No inventory, appraisal or estate account naming enslaved people is in the file, apart from the 1840 sale.
* No deeds or transfers involving Woodley were found. Deed books were not searched in this pass.

**(6) Was Woodley sold, transferred, inherited or removed before 1840?** **Undetermined.** Possibilities the
records allow, none of them evidenced:
* sale by Adin Lynch between 1811 and 1839
* a gift or advancement to one of his children at marriage, e.g. Martha (Dudley House), Polly (Chas. D.
  Buckner) or the Laffoon daughter. Such gifts would not be in an estate division
* bequest by name in the will
* hire elsewhere
* escape
* death

**(7) Documented migration to Louisiana / Catahoula.**
* No document found connects the Lynch, Jones, House or Dromgoole families of these cases to Louisiana.
* The Catahoula records already collected (conveyance abstracts 1808–1839; 1810–1850 census transcriptions;
  1878 voter list; death index; cemetery listings) contain **no** Lynch/Linch, Jones-of-Brunswick, Dudley or
  Isaac F. House.
* The surname Laffoon appears in Catahoula **cemetery** listings (earliest dated burial: born 1850), and
  "Buckner, Fortune, LA, 1832" is on the 1878 voter list. These are common Southern surnames with no
  documented link to the Brunswick families, so they are recorded and not pursued.

## 4. Candidate identity assessment

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| The 1811 Woodley existed, belonged to Judith Jones's estate, and was allotted to Adin Linch on 23 Mar 1811 | **Confirmed** (img 0013, 0014, 0007) |
| He was a young child in 1811 | **Plausible interpretation.** Only the two lowest values (Woodley £15, German £18) fit young children, and he is absent from the 1790 will. No age is written anywhere |
| He was a child of Violet or Sary | **Unsupported hypothesis.** No document states parentage |
| He left Adin Lynch's ownership between 1811 and Feb 1840 | **Plausible interpretation.** He is absent from the complete at-death sale, but death or an unrecorded transfer equally fit |
| He went to Louisiana | **Unsupported.** No document |
| He is Woodley Drumgoole of Catahoula Parish | **Unsupported; not excluded.** Compatible with: Virginia birth c. 1804–09; Brunswick County; documented Dromgoole involvement (Edward as commissioner, Thomas as surveyor and auditor, a later Edward as commissioner in 1840 and as a Lynch minor's next friend); and absence from Lynch's estate by 1840. No document bridges 1811 to Louisiana |
| A Dromgoole owned him | **Not supported.** Every Dromgoole role in these files is administrative: commissioner, surveyor, auditor, next friend. None is ownership |

## 5. Unresolved questions

1. What does Adin Lynch's recorded will ("copy marked A") say? Does it name Woodley, or mention earlier gifts of
   enslaved people to his children?
2. Did Adin Linch/Lynch convey Woodley by deed (sale, gift or mortgage) between 1811 and 1839?
3. Do the Brunswick County personal property tax lists for 1811–1839 show a drop in Adin Linch's count of
   enslaved people that dates a transfer? These lists count, they do not name.
4. Are the Kirby (1846), Phipps (1847), Colley (1845–52) and Batte (1848) Woodleys adults of the right age? Any
   one of them could be the 1811 Woodley sold on. A man still held in Virginia in 1846–1852 would argue against
   identity with a Woodley in Louisiana by the late 1840s.

## 6. Single best next archival action

**Obtain Adin Lynch's will as recorded in the Brunswick County Will Book** (proved before the January 1840
term), together with any inventory and appraisement recorded with it.
* **How:** Library of Virginia Archives Research Services (Brunswick County will book microfilm), or the
  Brunswick County probate records digitised by FamilySearch (sign-in required).
* **Why this document:** the chancery file says it exists and was recorded, but did not keep the copy. It is
  the most direct surviving statement of what Adin Lynch held and gave away before his death. It may name
  Woodley, or record advancements to his children that explain Woodley's absence in 1840.
* **If the will is silent,** the next step is the Brunswick County deed-book grantor index under Linch/Lynch,
  1811–1839.

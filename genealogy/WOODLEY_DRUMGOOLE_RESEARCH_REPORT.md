## 2. Established facts (from documents)

1. **1878 voter registration, Catahoula Parish (LAGenWeb transcription):**
   * `Drumgoole, Woodley | VA | 1804`
   * `Drumgoole, General | LA | 1850`
   * `Drumgoole, Rizella | LA | 1855`
   * `Drumgoola, Mat | LA | 1802`
   The "1802" for Mat is very likely a transcription error. The parish death index gives Mat Drumgole's
   death on 1 Jan 1929 at age 75, i.e. born c. 1853–54.
2. **Catahoula death index (transcription):** Mat Drumgole (d. 1929, age 75); Riszener Drumgole (d. 1929,
   age 76); Clara Evans Drumgoole / Drungoale (d. 1922, age 46).
3. **Brunswick County Chancery 1811-003** (Library of Virginia; images read):
   * *Decree, August Term 1810 (image 0005):* "Patsy J Brock (by Christopher Thrower her next friend)
     against Adin Linch and Salley his wife late Salley Jones … decreed and ordered that **Edward Dromgoole**,
     Willm Harrison, John P James and Claiborne House or any three of them do make an equal Division of the
     estate whereof Judith Jones died seized … and … the land devised … by the last will … of Harwood Clary
     dec'd to Judith Jones for life and remainder to Polley & Salley Jones …"
   * *Lot No. 2, Adin Linch (image 0013):* Butcher £110 · Solomon £90 · Joe £70 · Violet £55 ·
     **Woodley £15** · Lucey £95 · German £18 · Nelley £50 = £503.
   * *Lot No. 1 (images 0015–0016):* "drawn by Christr. Thrower guardian of Patsey J. Brock as her share of
     the Negroes belonging to the Estate of Judith Jones dec'd". It holds Isham, Brayboy, Jenney, Beckey,
     Doctor, Phill, Sarah and Anthony.
   * *Commissioners' report (image 0007):* "… have this day proceeded to make the division of the Estate of
     Judith Jones dec'd … Given under our hands this **23d day of March Eighteen hundred & eleven**. Willie
     Harrison, **Edward Dromgoole**, Claiborne House, Commissioners."
4. **Brunswick County Chancery 1850-003, Grief H. Lynch etc. v. Exr of Adin Lynch** (images 0009, 0010, 0015
   read):
   * The enslaved people of Adin Lynch's estate were sold around 1840; land-sale notes fall due February 1841
     and 1842.
   * Joe, German, Solomon, Butcher, Nelly with Lucy and Jane, Violet, Peggy, Susan, Tillman and Allan are
     listed with buyers and prices.
   * **Woodley is not among them.**
   * The commissioners were Jas. P. Harrison, **Edward Dromgoole** (a later Edward; the elder died 1835),
     William Harrison and Isaac F. House.
5. **27 Brunswick County chancery suits** with Dromgoole parties (1823–1917) are indexed. **None indexes an
   enslaved Woodley, Woody or Woodly.** Many, including all the 1848–1853 suits over Gen. George C.
   Dromgoole's estate, have no enslaved names indexed at all.
6. **Newspapers (Chronicling America, verified OCR sample):** Dromgoole references are J. P. Dromgoole & Co.
   patent-medicine advertising (Memphis) and Virginia political coverage of Gen. George C. Dromgoole,
   including his death in 1847. There is no Woodley and no Catahoula Dromgoole.

## 3. Inference (labelled as such)

* **Timeline fit of the 1811 Woodley.** He was a low-valued child in 1811. That is consistent with births
  in the range implied by the censuses and voter list (1804–1809).
* **He left Adin Lynch's household between 1811 and about 1840.** The rest of his 1811 lot appears in the
  c. 1840 sale and he does not.
* **Louisiana timing.** If General (born Louisiana c. 1846–1850) is Woodley's biological son, born where
  Woodley lived, then Woodley was probably in Louisiana by the mid-to-late 1840s. A departure from Lynch
  before 1840 would fit. This is a compatibility argument, not evidence of a sale or migration.
* **The Dromgoole link is real but indirect.** Two Edward Dromgooles served as commissioners dividing
  property in this Jones–Lynch family, in 1811 and c. 1840. The House family (Claiborne, Isaac F., Dudley)
  appears in both, and Isaac F. House also sued Gen. G. C. Dromgoole's estate in 1848. These families
  belonged to one Brunswick County social and legal network. That makes it plausible that an enslaved man
  from it later used the Dromgoole name. It does **not** show that a Dromgoole owned him.
* **Surname choice.** Freedpeople did not always take a last enslaver's surname. "Drumgoole" could reflect
  an earlier owner, a parent's enslaver, a community association, or a choice made for other reasons.

## 4. Contradictions and cautions

* **1880 census** (as transcribed): father's and mother's birthplace "Louisiana". That conflicts with a
  Virginia-born man born c. 1804 to Virginia parents. It is probably an enumerator or transcription error;
  check the original image.
* **Age drift:** 61 in 1870 (born 1809), 76 in 1880 (born 1804), 1804 in 1878. Ages of formerly enslaved
  people are often uncertain by ±5 years or more.
* **Name frequency:** at least 9 Virginia chancery suits index an enslaved "Woodley". The Powell suits
  (Brunswick, will of 1791; suits 1798 and 1823) show a Woodley alive by 1791–1798, probably too old unless
  ages were badly understated. Suits from 1847–1849 (Phipps, House) show Woodleys still in Virginia then.
  Those can't be your ancestor if he was in Louisiana by then, unless the suit narrates earlier events.
* **Anthony / Polly coincidences** (Lot No. 1 in 1811; Robinson v. Dromgoole in 1841) are noted but carry
  no weight. Both names were common.

## 5. What was not searched (and why)

* **FamilySearch** (Freedmen's Bureau collection 2333781; Full-Text Search of Catahoula conveyance and
  succession books): requires sign-in.
* **Ancestry, Fold3, Newspapers.com:** paywalled.
* **Duke (George Coke Dromgoole Papers), William & Mary (Dromgoole Family Papers), UNC, SNAC finding aids:**
  automated access blocked by bot challenges. Only search-engine summaries were seen. They mention plantation
  records, Brunswick County court papers and an 1821 tax receipt for "4 slaves over the age of 16".
* **Library of Virginia Virginia Untold, Louisiana State Archives, Louisiana Digital Library, Catahoula Parish
  clerk (conveyance and succession books after 1839):** not reached in this pass.
* No claim is made about any of these collections' contents.

## 6. Answer to the closing questions

* **Strongest new document discovered:** for identification, the 1878 Catahoula voter registration entry
  "Drumgoole, Woodley, VA, 1804". For the pre-emancipation search, Brunswick County Chancery Cause 1811-003,
  Lot No. 2 (23 March 1811): an enslaved child "Woodley", £15, allotted to Adin Linch, with Edward Dromgoole
  as commissioner. It is a **possible candidate**, not a confirmed identification.
* **Is Woodley's Virginia birthplace established?** No. Virginia (state) is consistently reported (1870,
  1878, 1880). Brunswick County is a documented, testable hypothesis, not a finding.
* **Arrival in Louisiana:** not documented. It is bounded only indirectly: by the c. 1840 Lynch estate
  sale, if the 1811 Woodley is him, and by General's Louisiana birth c. 1846–1850.
* **Enslaver identity:** not established. If the 1811 Woodley is him, his documented enslaver in 1811 was
  **Adin Linch (Lynch)** of Brunswick County, not a Dromgoole.

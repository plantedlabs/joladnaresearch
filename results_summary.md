# GGVP Jola Y-DNA Haplogroup Scan — Results Summary
## E-Z15174 Frequency Analysis in Gambian Genome Variation Project

**Date:** September 5, 2026  
**Researcher:** Kelland Drumgoole (YF133256)  
**Dataset:** Gambian Genome Variation Project, GWJ population (PRJEB3252)  
**Target haplogroup:** E-Z15174 (Jola-Fonyi / Mandinka lineage, Western Division, The Gambia)

---

## Summary Finding

**E-Z15174 is present in 3 out of 47 Jola males (6.4%) in the GGVP dataset.**

These 3 samples are the same individuals already confirmed as Y-DNA matches on the YFull tree. No additional E-Z15174 carriers were identified in the systematic screen of all 40 previously unchecked males.

---

## Method

Samtools mpileup was used to query 4 haplogroup-defining SNP positions on the Y chromosome in each male CRAM file:

| SNP | GRCh38 Position | Ref | Derived |
|-----|-----------------|-----|---------|
| Z15174 | chrY:4,202,358 | G | T |
| Z15234 | chrY:8,414,659 | C | T |
| Z15231 | chrY:14,719,978 | G | A |
| Z15232 | chrY:14,765,245 | T | C |

A sample was classified as E-Z15174 positive if the derived allele was present at majority frequency (>50%) across multiple reads at Z15174 and Z15234.

## Interpretation

### Haplogroup Frequency
E-Z15174 frequency in GGVP Jola males: **3/47 = 6.4%**

This low frequency has important implications:

**1. E-Z15174 is lineage-specific, not population-wide**
E-Z15174 is not a broadly distributed Jola haplogroup. It is carried by a specific subset of Jola families — likely concentrated in one or a small number of villages or clans within the Western Division.

**2. The three matched families are almost certainly from the same community**
Finding 3 carriers out of 47 total, with all 3 already clustering on the same YFull sub-branches, strongly suggests geographic and genealogical clustering. SC_GMJOL5309851 and SC_GMJOL5309804 share a common paternal ancestor from only approximately 375 years ago (TMRCA ~375 ybp on YFull).

**3. The slave trade connection is highly specific**
The ancestor who was enslaved and transported to Virginia in the late 1700s came from a specific Jola family line — not from the general Jola population. Identifying the village of origin for the three matched families would therefore directly identify the community of origin for the Drumgoole paternal line.

**4. No additional relatives found**
The 40 unchecked males represent the complete set of previously uncharacterized Jola males in the GGVP dataset. The absence of E-Z15174 in any of them confirms that the three known matches represent the complete picture available from this dataset.

---

## Limitations

- **Low coverage sequencing:** GGVP samples are low-coverage (~4-6x average). Some positions had insufficient depth for confident calling. Two samples (SC_GMJOL5309973, SC_GMJOL5309975) had no coverage at Z15234 — these cannot be definitively excluded but show reference alleles at all other positions.

- **Dataset scope:** The GGVP sampled 100 individuals total (47 male, 53 female) from the GWJ (Gambian Jola) population in the Western Division. This is a small sample relative to the total Jola population and may not fully represent haplogroup diversity across all Jola communities.

- **No Bainuk or Balanta samples:** Related coastal West African peoples (Bainuk, Balanta) who share ancient ancestry with the Jola have not been sampled at comparable depth. E-Z15174 may also be present in these populations.
